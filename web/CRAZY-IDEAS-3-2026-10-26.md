# Actually crazy ideas, batch 3 (2026-10-26)

Not add-ons to the current site. Standalone, strange, shareable. Each could be its own page, its own domain, or the one thing people screenshot. Rule kept: real data or physics only, nothing invented about you.

## The site as a physical thing

1. **The site is a darkroom you must work in.** Gallery opens as a red-lit room, pitch black. You carry real photos in trays: drag into developer, stop, fix, hang to dry. Over-develop and the photo blows out. Real chemistry timing (developer ~ 6 min, scaled to seconds). Finish = print saved to your device as a real JPG.

2. **Build the site as a lab bench.** One long bench, no pages. Pan sideways: beakers (skills), a Bunsen burner (current projects), a balance (the Analyst dashboard numbers weigh against each other). Pour one beaker into another and it genuinely navigates. Chemistry degree as the UI.

3. **A site that ages.** Real clock. Each day without any visit, the page grows a little dust, the horse sleeps deeper, the photos fade a notch. Visitors wipe the dust off with the cursor. You open the site after a month and it is visibly "lived in".

## The site as a game nobody expects

4. **Portfolio as a football match.** Ninety seconds. You are the ball. Pass between players who are actually sections (work, photos, sound, notes). Where the ball ends at full time decides the page you land on. Goal = contact form opens.

5. **Recruiter roguelike.** Press `~`: the CV becomes a dungeon crawl. Rooms = jobs and projects, enemies = real problems solved (from your own case notes), loot = the real metric you moved. Death screen shows "run again" and a link to hire you.

6. **Boss fight with your own dashboard.** The Analyst KPIs become a bullet-hell. Every bullet is a real number from the live feed; dodge them. Beat it and the DAX measure unlocks.

## Strange senses

7. **Hear the site's server.** Page load as a drum machine: each request is a hit, size sets pitch, latency sets swing. Visit once, get a unique 3-second beat. Save as a ringtone.

8. **Smell-o-vision (words only).** Hover a photo and a real-sounding scent note appears, assembled from the EXIF (time of day, month, GPS place type): "monsoon dusk, wet laterite, frying oil". Generated from data, not invented memories.

9. **Site you can only see with your phone's camera light.** On a phone, tilt to move a flashlight cone over a dark gallery wall, using the real gyroscope. Desktop gets the mouse lantern.

## Meta-weird

10. **The site that edits itself in front of you.** `?` opens a live diff: the page rebuilds itself from a blank file, replaying the real git history commit by commit at 100x. Visitors watch the portfolio get built, including the rejected concepts.

11. **Anti-portfolio.** One hidden page lists the real things that failed (the 4 rejected concepts, bugs, slow scores) with the lesson from each. Radical honesty as the memorable thing. Real material already in your repo notes.

12. **Visitor leaves a stitch.** A shared kantha cloth, one stitch per visitor, real timestamps, colour from visitor's local time of day. After a year the cloth is the traffic graph. Needs a tiny backend (one table).

13. **Chemistry text-to-sound.** Type your name; it is spelled in element symbols where possible (e.g. "Tuhin" = Tu? no, falls back to nearest) and each element's real emission-spectrum colour paints your name as a flame test strip. Free, shareable image.

14. **Match the hour in Goa.** The whole site is only ever as bright as Goa right now. Visit at 3am IST: it is nearly black, with one lit window. Real sun maths, no API.

## If I had to pick three to be remembered for
- **#1 darkroom** (most original, ties photo + chemistry)
- **#10 self-building replay** (proof of craft, uses real git)
- **#4 football-match nav** (instantly shareable)

## Honest costs
- #1, #4, #5, #6 are each a mini-project (1-3 days).
- #12 needs a backend and moderation.
- #13 symbol-fallback logic needs design; flame-test colours are real, so keep to elements with known colours.
- #6 and #5 risk clashing with the one-screen, calm tone. Make them opt-in secret pages.
