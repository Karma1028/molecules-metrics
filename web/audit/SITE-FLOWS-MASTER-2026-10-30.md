## 3. Seven complete flows

### P1 "The Painted Door" (L1 + F1 + D1-D4 + Layer 4 + Layer 5)
Flow: scroll the patachitra film (shortened for `/ig`, with the role line first for `/li`) → seal break → **Two Doors**: the screen splits into Artist and Analyst, each looping a Flow clip; tap one and it plays a 15 s mini-brief, then you are in that world → Analyst depth is case files and nine-box; Artist depth is gallery, sound, matchday.
Two Doors in detail (your earlier option 2): desktop is a vertical split, phone is top and bottom; each door has a headline, one real number or photo, a live element (Analyst: Scorecard months; Artist: now playing or last score), and a "step in" tap. Entry URL decides which door starts open (`/ig` Artist, `/li` Analyst). A persistent "Other side" tab remains in both worlds as the bridge.
LinkedIn path: role line, Analyst door, Scorecard, case file, one-pager, résumé. Instagram path: short film, Artist door, gallery, then a bridge card ("how I'd analyse my own roll").
Two sides tie: the doors face each other for the whole session; the bridge cards and the painted-to-machined hand-off.
Slices: (1) door + film using existing panels, (2) new panels and fork, (3) case files, (4) cast and clips.
Best for: the story you described. Risk: the most assets and the longest build.

### P2 "The Metro" (L-scroll + F6 + D1-D4)
Flow: the landing is a scroll that rides a metro map from Bankura through Kolkata and Jaipur to Goa (his real stations); the Artist line (orange) and Analyst line (blue) run in parallel and **cross at interchange stations**: "Football analytics", "Photos as data", "Listening data". At each station a Flow still or animation appears and the station name opens its page. End of the line = Goa, now, with the fork as the final platform.
LinkedIn path: ride the Analyst line (BE, Celebal, InfoLeap, PGDM), tap a station for the case, résumé at the terminus. Instagram path: ride the Artist line (places, photos, matches), the interchange stations show the analysis behind them.
Two sides tie: the interchange stations are literally the projects that belong to both lines. The strongest visual proof that the two halves are one person.
Cost: low art cost (SVG map, the data is verified), medium build. Risk: a timeline is less cinematic than a film; needs one good animated train moment.
Reuse: `journey-stations.ts` (real stations with coordinates), thread/needle device, route loader line.

### P3 "The Bag" (L5 + F2 + D1-D4)
Flow: a 10 s muted trailer ends on his bag; it unzips and the objects spill out as the menu (camera, headphones, ball, notebook, laptop, résumé, letter). Pulling an object out expands it into its page (shared-element transition). Front pocket is labelled "Work", main compartment "The rest".
LinkedIn: laptop and résumé are largest, one tap each. Instagram: it is the "what's in my bag" format they already know.
Two sides tie: one bag, both kinds of contents. Cost: props art (free Flow stills) plus transitions. Risk: object illustrations must match the Ghibli look; reads casual to some recruiters.

### P4 "The Proof Reel" (L4 + L6 + D1-D6)
Flow: 20 s showreel of three animated case machines with real numbers → **Ask** input with chips ("Can he do my job?" opens Fit Check) → case files → Mekko, nine-box, driver tree. The Artist half is the last chapter, "Off the clock"; `/ig` swaps the reel for a photo and match reel and puts "Off the clock" first.
Two sides tie: bridge cards and the chapter order flip by door.
Best for: hiring outcomes; the most consultant-like. Risk: the weakest for Instagram; needs employer-data clearance for case numbers.

### P5 "The Wrapped" (L3 + L2 + F9 + Layer 5)
Flow: a swipeable year-in-data recap generated from live data (the pipeline already bakes Spotify, frames, commits, Barça), 8 cards, each card tappable into its page. Cards export as Instagram carousel, story and LinkedIn document. A new recap each month (the Magazine idea) gives a reason to come back.
Two sides tie: the recap mixes both halves in one story: "12 months at work, 33 frames, 21,000 minutes".
Cost: medium; the export tool is the new part. Risk: a recap that does not refresh looks dead; needs the Action to keep running.

### P6 "Match Day" (F8 + L5 + football depth)
Flow: kickoff; the 90-minute clock runs along the top; first half is the Artist side, half-time is the bridge (the Mekko/2x2 of Barça data), second half is the Analyst side, full time is Contact. Highlights jump to five moments.
Best for: the football-analytics angle and the Instagram football crowd. Risk: a gimmick if the visitor is not a football fan; keep it one flow among others, not the whole site.

### P7 "The Table" (F4 + D1-D4)
Flow: a periodic table of about 30 elements fills the screen with a gentle bonding animation; groups are coloured Analyst, Artist or Both; scroll zooms into the group you hover or tap. Each element is a page or project with a symbol, number and one-line fact.
LinkedIn: scan the whole thing in one glance, tap Analyst group. Instagram: elements carry photo tiles and song glyphs.
Two sides tie: "Both" is its own group. Cost: low-medium (grid, cells, zoom). Risk: grid-of-boxes can feel templated unless each element is hand-drawn; the chem-eng link is a theme, not an engineering-drawing style.

### Swappable forks (use inside any package)
F3 Desk/Room, F5 Radio dial, F7 Sticker album can replace the fork of P1, P3 or P4 without changing anything else.

## 5. My recommendation
- **Best fit to what you described: P1**, with **P2's interchange idea borrowed** inside Two Doors (the bridge cards become "interchange stations"). It keeps the scroll film with videos and Flow images, the two sides, and deep data.
- **Best cost-to-impact: P2.** Needs the least new art, uses verified data, and shows the two halves as one person better than anything else.
- **Best for pure hiring: P4**, but only as the Analyst depth inside P1, not as the whole site.
- **Best as a content engine: P5**, layered on top of any of them.
My rule from your history (a built concept was rejected on 2026-10-05): do not build the winner yet. First make **low-fidelity clickable mockups of the top three** (P1, P2, P3) with free Flow stills and a quick Stitch/HTML pass, so you can react to something you can see before any real build.
